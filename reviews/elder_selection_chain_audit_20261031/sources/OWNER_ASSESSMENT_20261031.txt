The project needs to make one major transition: from a system that is increasingly good at checking its own work to one that can demonstrate, independently and reproducibly, that its central mathematical claims are true.

Based on the issues identified in the previous investigation, I would prioritize seven changes.

1. Close the gap between the formal proof and the actual theorem

Highest priority

The most important weakness is the gap between abstract estimates and the actual Gaussian-field persistence problem.

For example, the formal countermodel we discussed showed that an upper-selection estimate does not establish a positive lower rate for genuine elder-rule pairings.

What needs to change:

* Define the actual measurable event that says a candidate pair is an elder-rule pair.
* Prove that the event satisfies the required lower and upper bounds.
* Connect every abstract probability or counting estimate back to that event.
* Make explicit which assumptions are needed at each step.

Why: A mathematically valid estimate about a proxy event does not automatically prove a statement about the actual persistence pairing. This is the central logical gap to close.

2. Resolve the extra-critical-point problem

Highest priority

The investigation identified a case where an additional critical point can prevent a proposed maximum-saddle pair from being the actual elder-rule pair.

What needs to change:

* Replace raw critical-point counting with a topological witness.
* Establish conditions under which a candidate pair really survives the elder-selection process.
* Bound the probability of configurations that violate those conditions.
* Show that the good configurations contribute at the claimed leading order.

Why: Counting candidate maximum-saddle pairs is not the same as counting actual persistence bars. Until this distinction is controlled, the leading asymptotic argument remains vulnerable.

3. Finish the unrestricted parameter estimates

The project has made progress on compact parameter ranges, but those estimates do not automatically extend to all relevant birth heights and gap parameters.

What needs to change:

* Complete the small-gap estimates.
* Prove the large-birth and large-gap tail bounds.
* Control the intermediate separation scales.
* Handle collisions and boundaries of the rank charts.
* State precisely which constants depend on dimension, covariance, and domain size.

Why: The asymptotic coefficient requires controlling the entire parameter space, not merely the region where the local calculation is easiest.

4. Separate proven results from research candidates

Some developments, such as the proposed next-order expansion and rejected-density estimate, are interesting but are not yet established results.

What needs to change:

Give every mathematical claim an explicit status:

Status	Meaning
Proved	Complete argument with checked dependencies
Proved under assumptions	Valid only under clearly listed hypotheses
Conditional	Depends on an unresolved lemma or theorem
Conjectural	Plausible, but not established
Refuted	A counterexample or contradiction has been established

Keep conjectural coefficients and numerical estimates out of the headline theorem until their derivations are complete.

Why: This prevents a plausible research direction from gradually becoming treated as a proven fact through repeated documentation and review.

5. Introduce genuinely independent verification

The existing automated reviews and audits are useful, but repeated review by related models or the same provider does not establish independent mathematical validation.

What needs to change:

* Obtain a review from a specialist in Gaussian random fields.
* Obtain a separate review from someone experienced in persistent homology and stochastic topology.
* Give reviewers the theorem and proof without the project’s preferred interpretation.
* Ask them to identify the weakest indispensable lemma and construct counterexamples.
* Publish the responses and unresolved objections.

Why: A system can become very good at detecting errors that its own review process knows how to look for while missing errors outside that framework.

6. Make the software prove its own coverage, not just pass its tests

The recent engineering work on module ownership, mutation tests, and failure-reason checks is useful progress.

But passing tests does not prove that every important mathematical claim is represented or that every dependency is checked.

What needs to change:

* Require every theorem to have a machine-readable dependency list.
* Link each dependency to its exact source, proof, and assumptions.
* Check that no theorem silently relies on an unverified declaration or imported result.
* Make incomplete proof obligations visible in the main status report.
* Preserve failed test runs and record why they failed.

Why: This makes it harder for a polished software pipeline to create a false impression of mathematical completeness.

7. Make self-improvement measurable

The project appears to be improving its organization and audit procedures. The next step is to measure whether those improvements actually increase mathematical reliability.

Track things such as:

* Number of unresolved proof obligations.
* Number of counterexamples discovered and resolved.
* Number of claims downgraded or withdrawn.
* Number of independent reviews completed.
* Number of formalized results that match the intended mathematical statement.
* Number of failed verification attempts that led to substantive corrections.

Why: More commits, more tests, more documentation, and more model-generated reviews are not necessarily more mathematical progress. The important metric is how many genuine uncertainties have been eliminated.

The order I would follow

Recommended priority sequence

* Prove the actual elder-rule selection statement.
    Connect candidate pairs to genuine persistence pairs.
* Resolve the extra-critical-point obstruction.
    Establish a valid topological witness and control its failures.
* Complete unrestricted estimates.
    Close all parameter tails, intermediate scales, and boundary cases.
* Obtain independent mathematical review.
    Test the proof outside the project’s own review ecosystem.
* Formalize and audit the complete dependency chain.
    Ensure the formal result really matches the stated theorem.
* Only then promote stronger claims.
    Revisit next-order asymptotics and rejected-density estimates.

What success would look like

The project should eventually be able to answer five questions with concrete evidence:

* Does the claimed exponent hold for the actual persistence bars, rather than a proxy count?
* Is the leading coefficient derived from a complete and justified limiting argument?
* Are all parameter ranges and error terms controlled?
* Can an independent specialist reproduce and challenge the argument?
* Does the formal verification cover the exact theorem being advertised?

My overall assessment: The project does not primarily need more automation, more agents, or more elaborate review infrastructure. It needs to close the mathematical gap between local candidate-pair calculations and actual global persistence pairings.

That is the change most likely to turn an increasingly sophisticated self-auditing research system into a genuinely reliable mathematical research system.
