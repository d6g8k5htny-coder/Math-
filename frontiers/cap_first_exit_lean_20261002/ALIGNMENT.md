## 3. Non-vacuity and necessity, proved in Lean

The toy model is `toyF x = max (-|x|) (x - 2)` on `ℝ`, with:

- the cap `[-2, 1]`;
- `M = 0`, `b = 0`;
- `S = 1`, `s = -1`;
- `z = 3`;
- the ridge `t ↦ 3t`.

The proved facts are:

- `toy_capHyp` and `toy_saddle_strict`: the toy satisfies (C1)–(C3) and (C2⁺).
- `toy_death_level`: the death level is `-1`.
- `toy_needs_C2`, at `s = -3/2`: Lean proves that (C1) and (C3) hold, that (C2) fails (`f(1) = -1 > -3/2`), and that
  the maximin level is not `-3/2`.
- `toy_needs_C1`, at `b = -1/2`: Lean proves that (C2) and (C3) hold, that (C1) fails (`f(0) = 0 > -1/2`), and that
  the maximin level is not `-1`. The witness is the constant path.
- `toy_needs_C3`, at `s = 5`: Lean proves that (C1), (C2) and `b < f(z)` hold, that no ridge exists (every path starts
  at `f(M) = 0 < 5`), and that the maximin level is not `5`.

Each necessity theorem is one conjunction, so it certifies its own premises, not only its conclusion.

`ridgeToy_capHyp` and `ridgeToy_saddle_strict` instantiate `ridge_capHyp` and `ridge_saddle_strict` at SP's normalization, with `r = 1`, `E = ℝ`, `f(x, y) = x³/3 − x/4 − y²`, `h = 0` and `F = x² − 1/4`. The gap there is exactly `r³/6`, so all ridge hypotheses are jointly satisfiable.

`ridgeToy_joint` and `ridgeToy_slices` run the same toy through the slice route (v1.5). The joint derivative along `y = 0` is `D x = (x² − 1/4) · dx`, its transverse part vanishes, and each slice `y ↦ x³/3 − x/4 − y²` is `2`-strongly concave. With `w = 0`, `ω = 0` and `δ = 2`, the curvature condition reads `2/3 < 16`. Every input of `ridge_inputs_of_joint` and `ridge_capHyp_of_slices` is therefore jointly satisfiable, and the two theorems re-derive (C1)–(C3) and (C2⁺).

The ridge toy has `δ = 2`, below SP's `δ > rm(8m − 5) = 22`. So `spToyF (x, y) = x³/3 − x/4 − 12y²` is added: there `m = 2` (`f_xxx = 2`), `δ = 24`, `w = 0` and the gap is exactly `r³/6`. `spToy_capHyp` shows that every hypothesis of `ridge_capHyp_sp` holds there, so SP's normalized hypotheses are jointly satisfiable. `spToy_normalized` does the same for `ridge_capHyp_normalized`, with `G0 = x² − 1/4`, `|G0''| = 2 = m` and gap exactly `1/6`.

`spToyF` has `λ = 24 < 8rm² = 32`, so it does not meet (H1). `h1ToyF (x, y) = x³/3 − x/4 − 20y²` (v1.6) has a constant transverse Hessian `−40`, so `λ = 40 > 32`, its Hessian increments vanish and the gap is exactly `1/6`. `h1Toy_capHyp` shows that every hypothesis of `ridge_capHyp_H1` holds there. `h1Toy_ridge_differentiable` (v1.7) states every hypothesis of `ridge_differentiable` at `h1ToyF`, with `h = 0`, transverse gradient `−40y`, its derivative `−40 · snd`, `δ = 40` and `U = (−3, 3)`, as conjuncts of its proposition, and derives that theorem's conclusion from them. `h1Toy_source` (v1.8) meets every hypothesis of `ridge_capHyp_source` on the collar `ε = 1/2`, with joint derivative `(x² − 1/4, −40y)`, transverse derivative `−40 · snd` and `H = −40`, and the theorem then yields its ridge and, for `h = 0` (`F = x² − 1/4` meets L12), its cap at `M = (−1/2, 0)`. `h1Toy_C3` (v1.9) meets every hypothesis of `ridge_capHyp_C3`, with `D²f = diag(2x, −40)` and third derivatives `T a b = 2a₁b₁ · fst` (only `f_xxx = 2 ≥ 7/4` is nonzero), and the theorem yields its ridge and cap with no `hconv` supplied.

The toy lives on `ℝ`, which is not compact. So the older-peak theorems (`older_peak`, `elder_death_level_peak`) have
no concrete instance exhibited in Lean. Their extra hypotheses (compact, locally connected, continuous `f`) hold on the
torus.

The toy is one-dimensional, so its "saddle" `S = 1` is a local minimum, the index-`d−1` point for `d = 1`. The toy
illustrates the hypotheses; it is not a model of the planar cap.

names:

- the reviewer's provider and agent;
- the exact head commit;
- the `MANIFEST.json` SHA-256.

row of §4, it says whether the interface is stated correctly.
